# Test results, 2026-10-20 (scenario-fernwick)

Model: Claude Sonnet 5.5 through the API, five drafts per arm, nothing else in
context. Arms:

- **control** — no skill;
- **1.5** — the skill as it stood before this release;
- **v1** — the first round of edits;
- **v2** — the shipped text, with round 3's edits.

A separate agent graded each round blind. It saw the fact sheet, the checks
and the drafts, but never which arm wrote which draft. Each table counts the
drafts that failed each check, out of five.

## What each edit answers

- **Writing a fresh draft (the provenance sheet).** Four control drafts
  narrated a scene that never happened ("Most of us have tried the Sunday
  cram…"). Two 1.5 drafts invented specifics too: one promised "the October
  figures" and cited "the question we hear most", and the other gave the
  team "months" of work the sheet never mentions.
- **Absolutes about people.** Three 1.5 drafts made a "nobody" claim:
  "Nobody needs to see 800 words in a sitting," "Nobody has to decide whether
  they have an hour to spare," and "Nobody had told us the card screen was
  the problem."
- **Stretched figures.** A recall figure that covers only daily reviewers
  became "five minutes a day is enough to keep most of the words you learn."
  In round 2, two v1 drafts made the reverse move, attaching the all-session
  median to the daily reviewers; round 3's edit names both directions.
- **Didactic framing.** "We learned what asking too much costs" and "the
  lesson is the same one" followed a volunteered failure in two 1.5 drafts.
- **Data as speaker.** "The number tells us", "our data can't separate the
  two", and "the 81% does not promise" are the commonest false agents in every
  arm.
- **Verbless summary line.** One v1 draft closed on "Five minutes on nearly
  every day of a month, and about four words in five from two days ago still
  there."
- **Mood softeners** and **spec shorthand** never failed at baseline, so this
  release adds no entry for them.

## What still fails

Check 9 failed in four of five v2 drafts. That is three rounds of edits, the
limit set before testing began, so the release ships with the failure
recorded here. The remaining hits are data as speaker in headings ("Five
minutes a day: what our September numbers show") and in a post describing
itself ("This post explains why, and where our evidence runs out"). Round 3's
grader also counted "the figure describes people who…", which the catalogue
treats as a state verb (like "covers"), so check 9's count is an upper bound.
The pair test finds these clauses only when the writer runs it on headings
too. A future edit can say so and be tested against this scenario.
