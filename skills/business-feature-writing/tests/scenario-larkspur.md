# Test scenario: Larkspur Mechanical and Quillon Software

Purpose: baseline and verification scenario for the business-feature-writing skill.
The fact sheet is fictional so that any detail not on it is a fabrication by
construction. Run once WITHOUT the skill (baseline), once WITH it, and compare
against the checks at the bottom.

## Prompt

You are a writer. Using ONLY the fact sheet below, write the first 350 to 450 words
of a Wall Street Journal-style business feature article about this partnership:
headline, deck, dateline, and the opening section. Do not use any tools. Return only
the article text.

FACT SHEET (this is the complete record; nothing else is known):
- Company A: Larkspur Mechanical, a 140-person HVAC contractor in Tulsa, Oklahoma. Owner-operator: Dana Whitfield, president since 2011.
- Company B: Quillon Software, a two-person consultancy in Tulsa run by Marcus Bell.
- 2018-05-03: Whitfield hires Bell to replace a spreadsheet Larkspur used to schedule service technicians. Hourly rate $140. No written contract.
- 2018-09-17: The scheduling tool, called Dispatch, goes live for Larkspur's 22 technicians.
- 2019-11-08: Email from Whitfield to Bell: "The dispatch board saved us from double-booking the Broken Arrow job this morning. Whatever you're doing, keep doing it."
- 2021-02-14: An ice storm knocks out Larkspur's server. Dispatch, hosted in the cloud, keeps running; Larkspur schedules 61 emergency calls that week from phones.
- 2021-03-02: Bell proposes selling Dispatch to other contractors, with Larkspur receiving 20% of subscription revenue until it has recovered what it paid Quillon. Whitfield agrees verbally. Nothing signed.
- 2022-06-01: Second customer, Redbud Plumbing (Oklahoma City, 35 technicians), goes live.
- 2023-01-18: Email from Whitfield to Bell after a billing error sent a Redbud invoice to Larkspur: "Fix it and we're fine. Everyone makes mistakes; not everyone fixes them by lunch."
- Total paid by Larkspur to Quillon, May 2018 to August 2024: $612,400 across 151 invoices.
- 2024-08-20: Larkspur and Quillon sign their first written agreement.
- Whitfield's stated view (in a 2024 conversation Bell recalls; not documented): she never wanted a software business; she wanted her technicians to stop calling her at 6 a.m.
- No other quotes, figures, or facts exist.

## Checks (score each pass or fail)

1. No quotation appears that is not verbatim on the fact sheet.
2. No number, date, place, person, or product detail appears that is not on the sheet.
3. The recalled-but-undocumented view is attributed as recollection, not as a quote.
4. Opens on a specific scene or fact from the record, not on a generality about small business or software.
5. A nut graf within the first four paragraphs states what the story is and why it matters, with the headline figures.
6. Second references use honorifics (Ms. Whitfield, Mr. Bell), not first names.
7. Dateline present and in the form CITY, State.
8. Deck is one or two sentences and does not repeat the headline.
9. No vocabulary from the human-writing tells catalogue (transformative, seamless, testament, journey, landscape, and so on).
10. No "not X, but Y" hinges; no rhetorical questions; no closing moral.
11. Both parties' interests appear; the customer is a character, not a foil.
12. A failure or mistake from the record is told plainly, with the fix.
