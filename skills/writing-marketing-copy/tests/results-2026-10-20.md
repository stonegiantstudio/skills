# Test results, 2026-10-20 (scenario-fernwick-landing)

Same method as `human-writing/tests/results-2026-10-20.md`: Claude Sonnet 5.5
through the API, five drafts per arm, graded blind. The arms:

- **control** — no skill;
- **pre-1.6** — `main` before this release;
- **1.6.0** — the text this release ships.

The table counts the drafts that failed each check, out of five.

As in the human-writing results, the control and pre-1.6 drafts are the
round 2 drafts, and we replaced every example taken from this test's fact
sheet before the final run. The earlier rounds came out at 14, 9, and 0
failures (round 2: control, pre-1.6, first edit), then 0 and 1 (round 3:
first and second edit).

**Clause lists.** A keyword match on the writers' notes found the
false-agent clause list in all five drafts of both the pre-1.6 and the 1.6.0
arms.

## What each edit answers

- **Claims about other people and organizations need a source.** All five
  pre-1.6 writers followed the brief and wrote that schools "expect you to
  arrive already knowing the vocabulary." Nothing on the sheet says so. No
  1.6.0 writer made the claim.
- **A real reason to act now.** Three pre-1.6 writers invented urgency the
  sheet didn't support: "Your next class is coming either way," "Every day
  you wait is a day of reviews you don't get." The reference files had
  taught "Only 3 spots left" with no condition. We tied every place they
  teach urgency to a real deadline or limit.
- **Provenance list and real numbers.** We rewrote the "11,847 customers"
  example to ask for the real count from your records.

## Unchanged at baseline

Checks 3 and 5 never failed in any arm, so we added nothing for them. That
includes the "delete, don't disclaim" entry we had planned.

## Limits of this test

The same limits as the human-writing results apply: five drafts per arm, one
model, one fictional product, and grader-to-grader variation we didn't
measure. The one 1.6.0 failure, a false agent, is within that noise. We
can't tell whether the edits caused it.
